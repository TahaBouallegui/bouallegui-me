Rebuilding DNS from scratch

B Bouallegui ·Mar 4, 2026 ·12 min read

Every engineer forgets how much they rely on DNS. It just works, or it just doesn't, and in the second case you're usually cursing while reading logs. This month I decided to stop cursing and rebuild a minimal resolver from scratch, one piece at a time, until I could explain exactly what happened to a single query.

Why start with the boring part

I began with the packet. A DNS query is barely more than a name, a type, and a class wrapped in a header. Reading the wire format by hand — hex dumping my own packets — was the first moment things clicked. Everything above it, the caching, the recursion, the retries, only makes sense once you can picture the bytes moving back and forth.

"The best way to understand a system you don't trust is to build a smaller one you do, then watch them argue over the same packets."

Then I built the caching layer. Recursive resolvers live and die by what they remember, and watching a local cache turn a dozen round-trips into one was a great lesson in why the boring optimizations are the boring optimizations.

Lessons so far

  • • Timeouts and retries are where real networks live.
  • • Caching isn't an optimization here; it's the feature.
  • • Breaking things at home is the fastest path to actually knowing.

This is a living writeup — I'll keep it updated as I rebuild each layer. If any of this is wrong, it's intentional: the point is to learn out loud, mistakes and all.
